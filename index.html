The problem: visible aging that basic moisturizers do not address

Skin naturally changes with age. Fine lines deepen, texture becomes less even, and skin can feel less firm. Many women in their late thirties and beyond look for products aimed at the appearance of wrinkles rather than just hydration. The challenge is that "anti-aging" is a crowded category where bold claims are common and proof is often thin.

That is why a product with a stated trial duration, participant age range and comparison against placebo is worth examining, as long as we read those claims with care.

What Kollagen Intensiv is

Kollagen Intensiv is a face cream sold by Skinception. According to the manufacturer, it was designed by Dr. Dave David, a Boston-based physician and cosmetic medicine expert, and is built around SYN®-COLL, a patented Swiss peptide. Kollagen Intensiv 84 day clinical trial results, explained

This is the heart of the product's appeal, so it gets the most scrutiny here. The manufacturer's page reports the following from its 84-day study:

  • A 354% improvement in the appearance of wrinkles compared with placebo.
  • A 201% improvement in skin texture compared with placebo.
  • Participants were women aged 35 to 55, using the ingredient for 84 days.
  • The study is described as conducted by an independent dermatological laboratory in Canada, using dermatological analysis with before-and-after imaging.

The page also lists improvements in firmness, elasticity and hydration among its key results, and links to a technical formulation document for the SYN®-COLL ingredient where the data is described.

How to read these numbers. The page attributes the 84-day figures to women using SYN®-COLL, the peptide inside the cream, and also presents them as evidence for Kollagen Intensiv itself. It is not made clear whether the trial tested the finished cream or the ingredient in a test formulation. A percentage improvement in measured "appearance of wrinkles" is also not the same as wrinkles disappearing by that amount. Treat it as the manufacturer's reported result, and open the linked study document yourself if the details matter to you. Individual results will vary.

Key ingredients and what the manufacturer says they do

The ingredient list on the product page is fairly long. These are the ones the manufacturer highlights, described here as its claims rather than independently verified facts:

  • SYN®-COLL: the patented Swiss peptide described as triggering fresh collagen production.
  • Palmitoyl Oligopeptide and Palmitoyl Tetrapeptide-3: peptides described as supporting skin repair and helping with inflammation-related and glycation-related aging.
  • Retinol: an antioxidant form of vitamin A the page links to skin cell renewal and collagen support.
  • Vitamin C forms (Magnesium Ascorbyl Phosphate, Ascorbyl Palmitate): described as supporting collagen production and brightening.
  • Hyaluronic acid, glycerol and shea butter: moisturizing and softening ingredients.
  • Green tea, cucumber and oat extracts: described as antioxidant and soothing.

The full list also includes cyclopentasiloxane, gluconolactone, soybean oil, tocopherol, dipalmitoyl hydroxyproline and others. Read the complete label before purchasing.

Important things to consider before buying

Clinical claims are the manufacturer's

The study figures come from the brand's materials. As noted above, clarify whether results relate to the ingredient or the finished product.

Retinol and sensitivity

Retinol is on the ingredient list. As general advice, not product-specific guidance, people with sensitive skin, or who are pregnant or nursing, often check with a doctor or pharmacist before adding a retinol-containing product. A patch test on a small area is a common precaution for any new skincare.

Allergens

The list includes soybean oil and oat extract. If you have related allergies, review the label carefully.

Patience and consistency

The manufacturer frames peak results at 84 days of consistent use. That is about twelve weeks, so expectations should be measured. Results vary from person to person.
